# Sponsorship Prospectus Source Notes

## Tin City Founders Constitution

Source: `/home/ubuntu/upload/Tin_City_Founders_Constitution.pdf`

The constitution positions Tin City Founders as a movement rooted in Jos, Plateau, established in 2026. Its stated culture is built around seven principles: showing up consistently in person, giving before taking, welcoming all founders across tech, trade, and student entrepreneurship, pairing serious ambition with serious fun, protecting trust inside the room, measuring real outcomes such as deals and business growth rather than noise, and putting Jos on the map through visible building. The document closes by framing Tin City Founders as built in Jos for the founders of Plateau.

## Built In Jos — The Founders Spotlight

Source: `/home/ubuntu/upload/BuiltInJos_Spotlight_Walkthrough.pdf`

The walkthrough presents a concrete inclusion mechanism for founders who are often underrepresented in startup ecosystems. It argues that the current founder map skews toward tech, agency owners, and students, while long-standing operators such as mechanics, supermarket owners, tailors, poultry farmers, and market women are also founders and should be recognized as core members of the entrepreneurial community.

The programme loop is described as a repeated cycle: nominate, visit, gift, feature, invite, and nominate again. The behavioural design emphasizes going to founders in their own place of work, talking instead of typing, giving first in visible ways, respecting the founder’s status, and inviting spotlighted owners to mixers as honoured guests.

The in-practice workflow is specific and replicable. A visit lasts roughly 5 to 20 minutes and involves two people, one speaking and one filming. The team arrives and asks three simple questions, takes a photo, gives a branded asset on the spot, asks for nominations, logs the details, and then publishes the founder’s story first to the founder’s own WhatsApp status, then to Tin City Founders, and then to Built In Jos.

## Tin City Founders Blueprint, Roadmap & Operating Model

Source: `/home/ubuntu/upload/Tin_City_Founders_Blueprint_Roadmap.md(1).pdf`

The roadmap recommends a governance model built on distributed ownership, one central convener, and public weekly commitments. It assigns five core domains: Community Steward, Growth and Outreach Lead, Marketing and Content Lead, Partnerships and Sponsorship Lead, and Operations and Programming Lead.

The near-term execution goal is explicit: launch a strong first event with roughly 30 to 40 founders in the room. The setup tasks include confirming venue and food, setting up Instagram and Luma, forming the WhatsApp community and founder card form, generating QR codes, running an “invite 5” growth engine, and completing a venue walkthrough and MC briefing.

The first 30-day success logic focuses on proving repeatable community ritual and collecting evidence. The document highlights attendance, Give/Ask exchanges, connection scoreboards, photos, and testimonials as the seed data for future sponsor outreach. It recommends watching show-up rate and repeat intent closely.

For months two to three, the roadmap becomes directly sponsor-relevant. It calls for building an international sponsor dossier from event data, registering a legal entity for contracts and grants, introducing showcases and a quarterly demo day, converting a local sponsor to renewal, and opening partner-program and credit conversations.

The sponsor-readiness section is particularly useful for the prospectus. It says international partners want to see a documented track record of events, a member registry, impact metrics such as connections, products shipped, jobs created, and funding raised, clear legitimacy signals including brand and online presence, and evidence that founders adopt partner tools. It proposes a two-track model: begin immediately by helping members access self-serve startup credits and ecosystem programmes, then progress after several documented events to community-partner pitches supported by a one-page dossier and a six-slide deck.
